Works Cited

Abbott, Daisy. "Choose Your Own Adventure! An Empirical Study on Gamification of Postgraduate Learning on Research Project Design." The Journal of Play in Adulthood, vol. 6, 2024. https://doi.org/10.5920/jpa.1475.

This study shows the benefits of a "Choose Your Own Adventure" game or narrative as a teaching tool for complex research-designed outcomes. Abbott identified how this non-linear teaching style can be a crucial element in developing student autonomy. This study proves that interactive narrative projects with branching stories help students navigate non-linear decision-making. Which is exactly the goal of this project. I want to break down these big life decisions into smaller, more digestible parts for the viewer. Rather than just asking the big question of what you are doing after college. The goal is to help guide the viewer down a path that most aligns with their interest and plans for the future.

"Build A 'Choose Your Own Adventure' Lesson From Scratch." YouTube, uploaded by John Spencer, 22 Aug. 2017, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=huH46a2Nh0A.

This video provides a practical guide on how to build an interactive narrative learning experience with branching narrative stories. It goes in-depth on how to frame certain questions with the user in mind — you don't want to lose the plot in the middle of the experience. The goal of a project like this is to keep it clear and concise. Another helpful tip from the video was to map out every possible ending before you start to make questions. This ensures that you always have a possible result in mind. Finally, it also talked about the power that comes from students taking ownership of their answers and learning from that.

Chart.js. Version 4.4.1, 2024, https://www.chartjs.org/.

I started to explore the possible libraries that I could use in order to display the data I get from each of the possible path results. I ended up finding this lightweight JavaScript library that displays visually minimalist charts. This could be a unique way to display the data at the end of the experience, so that the user can see all the various outcomes and what percentage they fall under for the specific category they ended up choosing. I think this would add a unique element to the page and would make the experience feel more dynamic compared to other quizzes that just display your results.
